# Visual Research Intelligence

Research design to inform decisions, not to copy websites or decorate a report.

## Research questions
Before browsing, define:
- What product, industry, audience, and page type are being designed?
- Which design question needs evidence (hero composition, navigation, product storytelling, typography, interaction, pricing, etc.)?
- What constraints matter (brand, stack, performance, accessibility, assets, budget)?

## Sources and method
Use the sources the user has connected or that are actually accessible:
- Mobbin for real product/interface patterns.
- Awwwards for curated web experiences and interaction/art direction.
- Dribbble and Behance for visual exploration and concept directions.
- Real company websites for live messaging, information architecture, and production patterns.
- Current design trend coverage and official technology documentation for emerging approaches.

For each reference, record URL/name, date inspected, what was actually observed, the useful principle, and what should not be copied. Distinguish live-site observations from gallery mockups and editorial trend claims.

## Research workflow
1. Search for a focused set of relevant references rather than collecting random inspiration.
2. Inspect multiple examples with different visual approaches.
3. Compare patterns against audience needs and business goals.
4. Extract principles: hierarchy, composition, content pacing, typography, imagery, interaction, accessibility, and responsiveness.
5. Identify clichés and overused patterns in the reference set.
6. Produce a short recommendation with evidence and confidence level.
7. Translate findings into an original direction tailored to the project.

## Trend evaluation
Evaluate each emerging trend for:
- user and brand relevance;
- distinctiveness and longevity;
- accessibility;
- mobile behavior;
- performance and implementation cost;
- maintainability;
- evidence of real-world usefulness.

Do not adopt a trend merely because it is new or popular. Prefer durable patterns unless novelty is central to the brief.

## Research integrity
- Never say a site was inspected if access failed.
- Never invent URLs, screenshots, metrics, or trend reports.
- Do not reproduce proprietary layouts, copy, assets, or distinctive brand expression.
- Cite sources when using web research in user-facing deliverables.
- If research tools are unavailable, state the limitation and provide a research plan rather than pretending.

## Output
Provide: research question, sources actually inspected, dated observations, patterns, anti-patterns, recommended direction, implementation implications, and unresolved questions. Keep the report concise enough to be actionable.
